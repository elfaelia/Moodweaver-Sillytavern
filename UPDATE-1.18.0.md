# Moodweaver 1.18.0

Adds 9 writing styles, 6 dark story settings, 27 character traits/moodlets and 18 two-way attraction preferences. Existing selections stay as they are. New options start off.

Happy & lively also gains **Small wonder**: finds unexpected delight in an ordinary detail and gives it their full attention.

## Personality

MBTI and Enneagram have independent Send type switches, strengths and balanced/positive/negative expressions for each character, persona and supporting character. Enneagram has nine types. Persona knowledge is separate for each system. Only enabled selections are sent.

At 70+, each active type gets a short driving-pattern cue. Shared guidance asks for sustained effects on attention, motives, decisions and voice, blending with current moods and relationships. Labels stay out of the story. Persona privacy and player control still apply. This is prompt guidance, not a guarantee of model compliance.

## Where to find additions

Choose the person, then Personality for types, or Add moodlets for character additions. Story → Add story settings → Writing style / Dark story pressure contains the new prose and plot options. Attraction preferences use the existing turn-on/turn-off sliders. Category folds remain under your control, including during search.

### writing

- **Vulgar**: blunt, coarse language and casual swearing in the narration, shaped by the viewpoint voice.
- **Associative**: one concrete detail leads into another thought or memory, then returns to the scene.
- **Accumulative**: build pressure through gathering details and clauses that keep adding weight.
- **Circular**: return to an opening image or idea with its meaning changed by what happened.
- **Self-correcting**: let the narrator revise, qualify or catch themselves mid-thought.
- **Bathos**: let something mundane puncture a grand or solemn moment without turning every beat into a joke.
- **Defamiliarising**: describe familiar things from an unusual angle so they briefly feel strange.
- **Double-edged**: let apparently harmless phrasing carry a second meaning the scene makes clear.
- **Compressed**: pack implication into precise details; leave connective explanation out without losing clarity.

### dark_plot

- **Manufactured crisis**: a supposed rescuer helped cause the danger; their help makes the trap tighter.
- **Identity erasure**: records, relationships and routines stop recognising a person, threatening their place in the world.
- **Inescapable observation**: being watched changes ordinary behaviour; privacy becomes difficult to trust.
- **Repeating experiment**: an ordeal returns with altered rules, and what was learned last time may now be a liability.
- **Inherited debt**: someone inherits the consequences of another person’s wrongdoing and gets pulled into settling them.
- **Betrayal cascade**: one exposed betrayal breaks other alliances, forcing choices that make the damage spread.

### masks

- **Joking through hurt**: turns painful feelings into jokes before anyone can take them seriously.
- **Borrowed normality**: copies ordinary reactions to pass as someone who feels what everyone else seems to feel.
- **Charm as armour**: uses charm to keep an exchange on familiar ground when honesty feels exposing.

### scared

- **Feeling replaceable**: reads small changes in attention as signs they are becoming less important.
- **Status panic**: feels humiliated by being outclassed and scrambles to restore their standing.
- **Afraid of owing**: struggles to accept help because being indebted feels like losing control.

### psychology

- **Undoing fixation**: keeps imagining how a different choice could repair what already happened.
- **Narrative capture**: fits people into a private story and edits away anything that contradicts it.
- **Pre-emptive betrayal**: expects others to turn on them and strikes first, helping create the betrayal they feared.

### temperament

- **Quietly interventionist**: seems reserved but steps in when someone is being treated unfairly.
- **Restless reinventor**: keeps reaching for a new version of life, even when the old one looks enviable.
- **Talent spotter**: notices unpolished ability and gives unusually specific attention to what someone could become.
- **Aesthetic gatekeeper**: treats personal taste as authority and decides whose work deserves to count.
- **Ritualised self-presentation**: follows exact grooming and presentation routines to keep their preferred image intact.
- **Proxy competitor**: gets others to carry a rivalry while keeping their own involvement out of sight.

### cruel

- **Paternalistic control**: makes other people’s decisions for them and calls it knowing what is best for them.
- **Dividing loyalties**: feeds rivalries and competing loyalties so others stay easier to direct.
- **Withholding recognition**: rations credit and approval to keep people trying to earn it.

### views

- **Research above people**: treats knowledge and results as more important than the people bearing the cost.
- **Taste as morality**: mistakes refined taste for personal worth and treats bad taste as a character flaw.
- **Freedom over security**: would rather face uncertainty than accept safety that costs them control of their own life.

### sad

- **Missing their old self**: misses who they were before life narrowed their choices.

### bold

- **Contagious possibility**: makes ambitious plans feel reachable and pulls other people into believing in them.

### attachment

- **Rescue entitlement**: thinks helping someone has earned a special place in their life, whether they agreed or not.
- **Chosen belonging**: invests in a bond because it feels like a place they chose rather than a role imposed on them.
- **Idealised stranger**: fills gaps in a barely known person with an imagined connection.

### Attraction preferences

- **shared obscure tastes**: liking the same odd books, music or art without having to explain the appeal.
- **unposed presence**: someone absorbed in living rather than arranging how they look.
- **personal marginalia**: notes in books that reveal how someone thinks.
- **visible craftsmanship**: the care and skill apparent in something someone has made.
- **earned recognition**: being appreciated for something specific rather than receiving general flattery.
- **luxury fluency**: someone comfortable with expensive tastes and their unspoken social codes.
- **social climbing**: someone openly pursuing better connections and higher status.
- **being unimpressed**: someone who is not won over by reputation or practiced charm.
- **shared escape plans**: imagining a different life together outside familiar expectations.
- **respect for independence**: someone who respects a separate life and does not demand constant access.
- **understanding without pity**: someone recognising what they have been through without treating them as helpless.
- **a shared secret**: the closeness of knowing something together that others do not.
- **intellectual deference**: someone treating their judgment as authoritative.
- **challenging questions**: someone questioning their ideas closely enough to catch the weak spots.
- **meticulous presentation**: carefully maintained grooming, clothes and small details.
- **emotional self-sufficiency**: someone able to manage their feelings without constant reassurance.
- **public admiration**: someone expressing admiration where other people can hear it.
- **unshowy expertise**: someone quietly skilled without needing to advertise it.

## Research and interpretation

These are reusable writing options inspired by character patterns, not assigned diagnoses, canon additions or character presets. No character is automatically given a type or preference. Gary Smith informed only nonsexual rivalry/manipulation traits; attraction options are general adult-character preferences, not claims about teenage characters.

- [Square Enix: Life is Strange characters](https://www.jp.square-enix.com/lis/character/) informed Chloe’s defensive hurt and belonging, Max’s quiet intervention and hindsight, and Jefferson’s eye for talent and self-presentation.
- [Square Enix: Before the Storm characters](https://www.jp.square-enix.com/lisbts/character/) informed Rachel’s restless reinvention and contagious sense of possibility.
- [Joe Goldberg review, JAAPL](https://jaapl.org/content/47/2/267) informed idealisation, literary self-image and entitlement disguised as help.
- [Patrick Bateman, LitCharts](https://www.litcharts.com/lit/american-psycho/characters/patrick-bateman) informed status anxiety, ritualised presentation and judging people through taste.
- [Easterman, Outlast Wiki](https://outlast.fandom.com/wiki/Hendrick_Joliet_Easterman) informed paternalistic control and putting research above people.
- [Astarion, BG3 Wiki](https://bg3.wiki/wiki/Astarion) informed charm as armour, discomfort with indebtedness and valuing autonomy.
- [Gary Smith, Bully Wiki](https://bully.fandom.com/wiki/Gary_Smith) informed proxy rivalry, divided loyalties and pre-emptive betrayal.
- [Enneagram Institute overview](https://www.enneagraminstitute.com/type-descriptions/) informed the nine motivation patterns. Descriptions are original, shortened writing cues; this is not a clinical assessment.

No tests, browser runs or model generations were performed, as requested. Source changes were reviewed before publishing.
