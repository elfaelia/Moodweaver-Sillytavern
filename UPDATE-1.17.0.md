# Moodweaver 1.17.0

90 new options: 9 intimate-writing styles, 2 confident moods, 8 attachment patterns, 2 sad moods, 3 masks, 6 attraction states, 8 roles, 16 kinks, 8 moral outlooks, 8 mental patterns and 20 two-way attraction preferences.

## Categories stay where you leave them

Saved categories no longer default to open. Searching hides non-matches and shows match counts on the remaining headings, but does not open or close them. The same applies to relationship categories. Your folds are remembered per person and menu for the current page session, including changes made while searching. Scrolling and slider edits do not expand categories. Clicking an active mood chip still opens that one category so its slider can be reached.

## Where to find the additions

Reload the SillyTavern page and check the panel footer says **1.17.0**. For each person, use **＋ Add moodlets** to browse the full catalogue; their saved list only includes what you add. Writing styles are under **The story → ＋ Add story settings → Smut style**. All new entries start at zero.

## Prompt wording

The new settings use the existing strengths and persona knowledge rules. Each enabled mood or role gets a short description; writing styles get a label and brief cue. Attraction preferences retain their -100 to +100 scale. Four potentially vague new preference labels get a short clarification in the prompt; existing preferences send the same wording as before. Nothing adds another general instruction block, and unselected entries add no roleplay-prompt tokens. The optional dynamic analyser still includes catalogue information in its separate requests.

Masculine and Feminine POV are embodied viewpoint lenses. They keep the established narrator and body rather than changing the characters. The descriptions do not prescribe a personality or treat one viewpoint as inherently more forceful or emotional. As with other story settings, strength controls emphasis, not a change of canon.

## Additions

### Smut style (9)

- **Masculine POV:** a male gaze grounded in his bodily sensations, physical presence and personal desires; keep the established narrator and anatomy.
- **Feminine POV:** a female gaze grounded in her bodily sensations, physical presence and personal desires; keep the established narrator and anatomy.
- **Dark:** intimacy coloured by secrecy, uneasy motives and the sense that closeness has consequences.
- **Gothic:** intimacy steeped in atmosphere, private rituals, old secrets and fascination with the uncanny.
- **Noir:** intimacy filtered through a jaded voice, mixed motives and sharp observation.
- **Dreamlike:** intimate moments drift through sensory associations and elastic time while physical events stay clear.
- **Deadpan:** intimacy told with a straight face; odd details and mismatched expectations carry the humour.
- **Awkward / realistic:** intimacy includes imperfect timing, mundane interruptions and people adjusting without polished choreography.
- **Decadent:** intimacy feels indulgent and excessive, with expensive tastes and appetite outrunning good judgment.

### Confident & driven (2)

- **Unapologetic:** owns their wants and choices without softening them to win approval.
- **In their element:** familiar ground brings out easy confidence and a sure sense of what to do.

### Attachment & obsession (8)

- **Limerent:** fixates on whether affection is returned, building hope and despair around small signs.
- **Rescue fixation:** needs to be someone’s rescuer and struggles when that person stops needing help.
- **Emotional scorekeeping:** counts who reached out, cared more or made the last sacrifice.
- **Jealous of their past:** dwells on a partner’s former connections and feels compared with people who are gone.
- **Merging identities:** blurs their own tastes, plans and identity into the relationship.
- **Idealise, then devalue:** swings between seeing someone as extraordinary and dismissing them when disappointed.
- **One-person exception:** gives one person loyalty and tenderness they rarely offer anyone else.
- **Attached to their potential:** clings to who someone could become and the imagined future together.

### Sad & hurt (2)

- **Unacknowledged grief:** carries a loss they cannot openly mourn or that others refuse to take seriously.
- **Resigned:** expects nothing to change and has stopped spending hope on it.

### Hiding & showing (3)

- **Strategic vulnerability:** reveals a carefully chosen weakness to disarm someone or shape their impression.
- **Overcompensating:** overplays a quality to cover the insecurity or feeling underneath.
- **Plausible deniability:** leaves intentions half-spoken so they can deny the meaning someone takes from them.

### Desire & attraction (6)

- **Admiration turning to desire:** respect for someone starts taking on a personal, romantic or physical pull.
- **Intellectual chemistry:** the way their minds meet makes discussion feel charged and personal.
- **Aesthetic fascination:** absorbed by someone’s appearance and presence without necessarily wanting a relationship.
- **Wants to be pursued:** wants someone to make an effort and show that their interest lasts.
- **Attraction to contrast:** differences in manner, background or appearance are part of what draws them in.
- **Competitive attraction:** being matched or challenged makes a rival more compelling.

### Roles & dynamics (8)

- **Living statue:** plays an artwork on display, with stillness, deliberate posing and presentation central to the role.
- **Wind-up doll:** plays a mechanical doll with stylised movements, set gestures and an imagined winding ritual.
- **Human furniture:** takes a staged furniture role, with holding a position and being useful central to the arrangement.
- **Ringmaster:** leads the dynamic like a theatrical act, giving cues and shaping when others take the spotlight.
- **Jester:** uses performance and licensed cheek to entertain, needle authority and earn favour.
- **Royal favourite:** plays the indulged favourite, trading charm and private influence for special treatment.
- **Confessor:** takes the listening authority role, inviting admissions and offering judgment or absolution within the play.
- **Penitent:** plays someone seeking absolution, with confession and symbolic acts of making amends.

### Kinks (16)

- **Masks:** drawn to concealed faces, altered identity and the contrast between disguise and familiarity.
- **Corsetry:** drawn to corsets, their sculpted silhouette and the formality they give clothing.
- **Stockings:** drawn to stockings, their texture, seams and the way they frame the legs.
- **Silk / satin:** drawn to smooth, glossy fabric and the way it moves against the body.
- **Clown attraction:** drawn to clown makeup, exaggerated performance and the mix of absurdity and unease.
- **Transformation fantasy:** drawn to imagined changes of form or identity, with the transformation itself part of the appeal.
- **Cross-dressing:** enjoys clothing associated with another gender as part of chosen intimate play.
- **Zentai:** drawn to full-body fabric suits, smooth outlines and the anonymity of a covered face.
- **Body paint:** drawn to the body becoming a temporary canvas through colour and painted patterns.
- **Burlesque:** drawn to theatrical teasing, costume and deliberately staged reveals.
- **Collaring rituals:** drawn to collars as chosen symbols of a bond, with their meaning and ceremony central.
- **Sploshing / wet and messy:** enjoys playful mess from food or other soft materials, with texture and ruined tidiness part of the appeal.
- **Agalmatophilia:** drawn to statues and mannequins, their sculpted forms and uncanny stillness.
- **Mechanophilia:** drawn to machines and vehicles, with their design, movement or sound part of the attraction.
- **Spectrophilia:** drawn to imagined ghosts or spirits and the idea of an intimate unseen presence.
- **Monster attraction:** drawn to fictional nonhuman forms and traits rather than treating them as flaws to overlook.

### Morals & views (8)

- **Moral absolutist:** believes some acts are right or wrong regardless of motive or circumstance.
- **Moral relativist:** judges right and wrong through context and perspective rather than one fixed rule.
- **Respectability obsessed:** treats looking proper in public as more important than what happens behind the scenes.
- **Puritanical:** distrusts indulgence and treats pleasure or loosened standards as moral failings.
- **Social Darwinist:** believes power proves worth and treats others’ struggles as evidence they deserve less.
- **Anti-authoritarian:** questions imposed authority and resents obedience being treated as a virtue.
- **Restorative-minded:** cares about repairing harm and changed behaviour more than punishment alone.
- **Fatalist:** believes the outcome is already set and reads choices through that expectation.

### Mind & psychology (8)

- **Confirmation bias:** notices what supports their existing view and discounts what challenges it.
- **Black-and-white thinking:** sorts people and situations into extremes, struggling to hold mixed judgments.
- **Emotional reasoning:** treats how something feels as evidence of what must be true.
- **Selective empathy:** understands and cares about some people’s feelings while shutting out others.
- **Self-mythologising:** turns their life into a private legend and edits inconvenient details to fit.
- **Sunk-cost fixation:** keeps investing because giving up would make earlier sacrifices feel wasted.
- **Uncertain identity:** struggles to pin down who they are apart from current roles and other people’s expectations.
- **Hypermentalising:** builds elaborate guesses about other people’s motives from small clues; those guesses can be wrong.

### Turn-ons & offs · Looks & body (5)

- **smile lines:** lines that deepen around the eyes or mouth when someone smiles.
- **facial asymmetry:** distinctive uneven features rather than a perfectly balanced face.
- **expressive eyebrows:** brows that give away amusement, skepticism or concentration.
- **calloused hands:** hands showing the wear of work, craft or practice.
- **dishevelled hair:** hair that has fallen out of place or never quite stayed tidy.

### Turn-ons & offs · Voice & senses (5)

- **precise diction:** carefully formed words and a distinct, deliberate way of speaking.
- **singing voices:** how someone’s voice changes when they sing.
- **unhurried speech:** someone taking their time with words and pauses.
- **ink and paper scents:** the smell of books, paper or ink on someone’s clothes or hands.
- **the scent of smoke:** a lingering smoky scent on someone’s clothes or hair.

### Turn-ons & offs · Personality & behaviour (5)

- **contradictory sides:** someone whose tastes or behaviour pull in unexpected directions.
- **personal rituals:** small repeated habits that feel particular to that person.
- **gallows humour:** finding humour in grim situations without making them less grim.
- **unusual fixations:** a person’s intense fascination with something obscure or unexpected.
- **social polish:** someone who knows how to handle a room and make an exchange look easy.

### Turn-ons & offs · Types (5)

- **performers:** people who command an audience through acting, dance or performance.
- **researchers:** people absorbed in investigating, questioning and finding things out.
- **craftspeople:** people who make or restore things with practiced hands.
- **provocateurs:** people who deliberately challenge taste, convention or comfortable assumptions.
- **outsiders:** people who sit outside a group’s norms or sense of belonging.
